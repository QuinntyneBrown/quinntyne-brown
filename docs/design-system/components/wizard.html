Wizard

A short form split into steps: a progress rail beside one card per step, with an optional live preview. Used by Submit an idea.

WCAG 2.2 AAStatus: stablenav + ol + form

Overview

Use a wizard when one form is too long to read at once and each step can be answered alone: Submit an idea has three steps (the problem, the idea, review). Use a plain form when everything fits on one screen.

  • The rail shows where the person is; future steps are disabled, finished steps link back.
  • Each step is its own .wizard-card with Continue in the footer.
  • The preview column shows the result as members will see it and appears only from 1280px.
  • Two steps are optional and an admin switches them on or off (Switch on Idea form), so the count is never hard-coded.

Anatomy

  1. Progress label.wizard-progress: "The problem 1 of 3" and a progress bar. Shown below 1024px in place of the rail.
  2. Step rail.wizard-steps in a nav; marker, title and an optional summary. The connector line turns accent once a step is done.
  3. Step card.wizard-card: body with a focusable h2, footer with Continue.
  4. Preview.wizard-layout__preview: a card with an icon-tile on a cover colour and placeholder copy until filled.

Variants

The layout is a grid that adds columns as space allows.

  1. 1The problem

What problem have you noticed?

Rail and card (1024px and up). Add wizard-layout--preview and an aside.wizard-layout__preview for the third column at 1280px.
HTML
<div class="wizard-layout wizard-layout--preview">
  <div class="sticky-aside"><nav aria-label="…"><ol class="wizard-steps">…</ol></nav></div>
  <form class="card wizard-card">…</form>
  <aside class="wizard-layout__preview">…</aside>
</div>

Three to five steps

Signed-out visitors always get three steps. Signed-in members also get each optional step the Idea form turns on, in order: "How far along is it?" then "Who do you need?" (the *-signed-in mocks, drawn with both on), so a member has three, four or five steps. The rail, the "N of M" label and the progress bar always count the same steps; nothing else changes.

Sizes

One size. The card body uses --space-6 padding, the footer --space-4 by --space-6, and the rail is 13.5rem wide.

States

Step states are real attributes, not data-state: aria-current="step", li[data-done] and disabled.

  1. 1The problem
  2. 2Your idea
Step 1 done, step 2 current, step 3 disabled. Done steps link back and fill on hover.
  • Invalid: Continue stays enabled; the field takes aria-invalid and an inline message (invalid).
  • Busy: the submit button on Review takes aria-busy="true" (busy).
  • Error: a danger alert at the top of the card keeps the answers (error).
  • Success: the static rail (.wizard-steps--static) with a sage icon tile (success).

Responsive behaviour

  • Below 1024px: one column; the progress label replaces the rail.
  • 1024px: rail and card side by side.
  • 1280px: the preview column appears when wizard-layout--preview is set; below that it is hidden, not stacked, because it only repeats what the form shows.
  • .choice-tiles--pair goes two across from 640px (see Radio group).

Theming

Only semantic tokens: the current marker uses --color-accent and --color-fg-on-accent, done markers --color-fg-accent, the static success rail --color-accent-subtle. Both themes pass the pairs below.

Accessibility

Role and pattern

A labelled nav holding an ordered list; the current step is aria-current="step". Each step is a real form. No tab roles: steps are a sequence, not peers.

Keyboard

KeyAction
TabRail links, then the form, then Continue. Disabled steps are skipped.
EnterSubmits the step (Continue).

Focus

On each step change focus moves to the card's h2 (tabindex="-1") so screen readers announce the new step.

Labelling

The rail has aria-label="Idea submission steps"; the progress bar is aria-hidden because the label says "1 of 3". The preview aside is labelled by its heading.

Motion

Marker fill transitions over --duration-fast, removed under reduced motion.

Touch

Steps are full-width rows; the rail shows only on desktop widths.

Content

  • Step titles are short noun phrases: "The problem", "Your idea", "Review".
  • The card heading is a question: "What problem have you noticed?"
  • Continue on every step but the last; the last says what happens.
  • No summary under a title unless the step needs explaining; the mocks dropped them.
